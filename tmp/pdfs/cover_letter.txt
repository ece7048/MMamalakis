Dear Members of the Selection Committee,
I am applying for the Group Leader - Computational Biology and AI position at the Cancer Research UK Manchester
Institute. My research asks how explainable, multi-scale AI can connect imaging and tissue phenotypes with the
cellular and molecular programmes that drive tumour heterogeneity and progression. I would establish a
programme focused initially on paediatric and adult brain cancers, combining methodological innovation in
foundation models and mechanistic interpretability with biologically grounded questions in imaging,
histopathology, single-cell and spatial data.
I am ready to make this transition because I have developed a coherent research direction, begun leading
multidisciplinary projects around it, and moved from applying established medical-imaging methods to designing
multi-scale learning systems. I am currently an Assistant Research Professor at the CRUK Cambridge Institute, a
Postdoctoral Transition Fellow in the CRUK Children's Brain Tumour Centre of Excellence, and an Affiliated Lecturer
in the University of Cambridge Department of Computer Science and Technology. My programme builds on nine
years of work across deep learning, medical imaging, explainable AI, neuroscience and computational biology.
Three achievements provide the foundation for the proposed group. First, I have developed and published
explainability and medical-imaging methods, including work on transparent AI in pulmonary hypertension, cardiac
image analysis frameworks, and stable explanations of deep networks. Second, I am leading and developing a large-
scale MRI foundation model trained on more than 425,000 scans spanning structural and diffusion imaging. The
project extends my work in representation learning, generative modelling and explainability and is planned for
release in summer 2026. Third, my recent cancer-focused work includes multimodal modelling in paediatric brain
tumours, a study of neuroimaging biomarkers following brain-tumour surgery, and collaborative Neuro-Oncology
work on longitudinal functional-connectivity markers in glioma.
My independent contribution is the intellectual thread linking these activities: explanations should be part of model
design and biological discovery, rather than an audit added after prediction. I have pursued this through attribution
stability, activation-gradient analysis, uncertainty quantification, representation learning and mechanistic
interpretability. At finer biological scales, I am developing approaches that connect gene-level features with cellular
and histological representations in paediatric brain cancer. These methods are intended to generate testable
hypotheses about tumour progression and candidate biomarkers; clinical or therapeutic utility would require
subsequent biological and clinical validation.
I currently lead and advise researchers across two principal research programmes. The gene-, cell- and histology-
level programme involves two PhD researchers and a bioinformatician in the Gilbertson laboratory. The MRI
foundation-model programme involves a postdoctoral researcher based at the University of Málaga, Spain, and two
PhD researchers from the University of Cambridge School of Clinical Medicine. I also co-supervise a Cambridge PhD
researcher in the Department of Psychiatry who works in neuroscience. Our research on geometry-guided
generative representations for functional brain graphs has been accepted at ICML 2026 (leading conference in
computer science). As Module Director and lead lecturer for Principles of AI-driven Neuroscience and Translational
Biomedicine (L205), part of the MPhil in Advanced Computer Science, I design the curriculum and teach, organise
and assess the module. Together, these responsibilities demonstrate my experience in coordinating concurrent
projects, mentoring researchers and advancing work from initial concept through validation to publication.
At CRUK MI, the group's distinctive question would be: which shared representations across imaging, tissue
architecture and gene regulation reveal mechanisms of tumour heterogeneity, progression and treatment
resistance that cannot be identified from one modality alone? The initial programme would build from my current

I have begun building the funding and research-infrastructure experience needed for independence. I have
received EPSRC and UKRI access to high-performance computing facilities, including an award for From MRI to
Multi-Omics: An Explainable Foundation Model for Brain Cancer, and I am a collaborator on two awarded biobank-
dataset access applications. I have also reviewed grant applications for EPSRC/UKRI, including the UK AI Safety
Institute call. At Manchester, I would use the group’s initial methodological and cancer-biology outputs to develop

structured to support applications to UKRI, particularly the EPSRC, for the multiscale AI framework I am developing.
The position therefore represents a timely next step: the opportunity to turn a coherent body of work in
explainable biomedical AI into an independent, focused cancer-research programme. I would bring to CRUK MI an
explainability-first approach to cross-scale modelling, experience coordinating multidisciplinary trustworthy AI
projects, and a commitment to developing researchers while working closely with cancer biologists and clinicians. I
would welcome the opportunity to discuss how this programme could contribute to CRUK MI's scientific strategy.
